  <section id="offer">
    <div class="num">01</div>
    <h2>The offer</h2>
    <p>Trades lose jobs not because they're outbid, but because a quote sits untouched until the homeowner calls the next name on the list. Job-management platforms like Jobber handle scheduling and invoicing — none of them tell you which quote needs a call <em>today</em>, or chase it for you.</p>
    <p>Quoteline is a pipeline tracker that surfaces exactly that: who to call today, ranked by urgency, plus the dollar value of quotes going cold. The sample data makes the case on its own:</p>
    <div class="stat-pair">
      <div class="fast"><div class="num-big tnum">65%</div><div class="lbl">close rate — followed up within 2 days</div></div>
      <div class="vs">vs.</div>
      <div class="slow"><div class="num-big tnum">14%</div><div class="lbl">close rate — followed up later, or not at all</div></div>
    </div>
    <p>Same jobs, same prices, in the sample data — only the follow-up timing changed. That gap is the entire pitch.</p>
  </section>

  <section id="pitch">
    <div class="num">03</div>
    <h2>The pitch</h2>
    <h3>Why not Jobber?</h3>
    <p>Jobber runs $29–249+/month before add-ons — realistically $300–400/month once a small team, users, and payment processing are factored in. It's a full job-management platform: scheduling, dispatch, invoicing. Most solo and small-crew operators only feel the pain of one specific thing it doesn't do — chase a quote — and don't need (or want to pay for) the rest.</p>
    <h3>How it works, in three steps</h3>
    <table class="steps">
      <tr><td class="n">1</td><td class="t">Send your quotes</td><td>Whatever they've got — a Jobber export, a spreadsheet, or nothing. They can start adding quotes by hand immediately.</td></tr>
      <tr><td class="n">2</td><td class="t">Set the rhythm</td><td>A 15-minute call to decide how fast follow-ups should fire, and what a review request should say.</td></tr>
      <tr><td class="n">3</td><td class="t">Live within a week</td><td>Delivered as a private link. No subscription required — the retainer is an add-on, not a gate.</td></tr>
    </table>
  </section>

  <section id="setup">
    <div class="num">04</div>
    <h2>Business &amp; tax setup</h2>
    <ul class="plain">
      <li>No formal registration is required to start: a sole proprietor can invoice under their own legal name in Canada.</li>
      <li>GST/HST registration is only mandatory once revenue exceeds $30,000 in a single calendar quarter, or over four consecutive quarters (CRA's "small supplier" rule) — optional below that.</li>
      <li>Track income for a T2125 filing at tax time regardless of registration status.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <section id="outreach">
    <div class="num">05</div>
    <h2>Outreach plan</h2>
    <p>E-commerce sellers are hard to reach cold (no storefront, hidden contact info, oversaturated with pitches). Trades solve all of that: public licensing registries, phone numbers on the truck, and a referral culture that rewards landing even one client.</p>
    <h3>Building the prospect list</h3>
    <table class="steps">
      <tr><td class="n">1</td><td class="t">City registry</td><td>Browse the <a href="https://dbaservices.calgary.ca/publicinq/Default.aspx?PossePresentation=LicensedTradeContractors&amp;PossePane=Table&amp;PosseObjectId=124631268&amp;ContractorType=All" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Licensed Trade Contractor List</a> by category (electrical, plumbing/gas). Confirms legitimacy — gives business name + neighborhood, not contact info.</td></tr>
      <tr><td class="n">2</td><td class="t">Google cross-ref</td><td>Search each business name + "Calgary" for their Google Business Profile — phone number, sometimes the owner's name, and a read on their digital presence.</td></tr>
      <tr><td class="n">3</td><td class="t">HomeStars check</td><td><a href="https://www.homestars.com/find-professionals/calgary-alberta" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HomeStars</a> overlaps heavily with the registry and shows phone + review count directly — active reviewers are warmer prospects, since they already care about winning leads.</td></tr>
    </table>
    <h3>Prioritize</h3>
    <p>Solo-to-5-person crews doing residential work. Bigger commercial-only outfits usually already run full Jobber/ServiceTitan setups with a dedicated ops person — a much harder sell.</p>
    <h3>First message (text or DM, not cold email)</h3>
    <blockquote class="script">
      Hey [Name] — built a quick tool for Calgary [trade] shops that flags which quotes are going cold. Ran it on some sample data: close rate drops from 65% to 14% once follow-up slips past 2 days. No pitch, just a live demo: [link]. If it's useful, I'll build your real version for $750–1,200 one-time — no subscription unless you want the automated-follow-up add-on.
    </blockquote>
  </section>
